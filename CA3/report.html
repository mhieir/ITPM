PROJECT OBJECTIVE

This project aims to simulate and evaluate the performance of the IEEE 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6) standard within the ns-3 simulation environment. By establishing a specific network topology, the behavior of the network is examined under varying conditions. The ultimate goal is to measure and analyze key performance metrics—such as Throughput, End-to-End Delay, Packet Loss Rate, and Jain's Fairness Index—to develop a deep understanding of the enhancements introduced by Wi-Fi 6 over its predecessor, Wi-Fi 5.

1. PHASE 1: IEEE 802.11AC (WI-FI 5) BASELINE

1.1 Topology and Architecture
To establish a baseline for comparison, a standard Star Topology was implemented. A single Access Point (AP) was positioned at the origin of the coordinate system. Five Stations (STAs) were deployed evenly along the perimeter of a circle with a 5-meter radius around the AP. To ensure a static network environment for controlled testing, the ConstantPositionMobilityModel was assigned to all nodes.

1.2 Protocol Stack Configuration

  • Physical Layer: Configured to establish a shared wireless channel, enabling realistic signal degradation and packet collision behavior.
  • MAC Layer: Configured according to the IEEE 802.11ac standard. The AP and STAs were assigned complementary MAC roles (ApWifiMac and StaWifiMac) sharing an identical SSID to establish connectivity using CSMA/CA mechanisms.
  • Network Layer: A complete TCP/IP stack was installed, with IPv4 addresses allocated within a shared subnet to facilitate routing.

1.3 Traffic Generation
Uplink traffic was generated using a UDP Echo application running for a duration of 10 seconds. The AP acted as the receiver, while the STAs acted as transmitters. Packets were transmitted at 0.5-second intervals. To observe the impact of varying payloads, STAs 1, 3, and 5 transmitted 1024-byte packets, whereas STAs 2 and 4 transmitted 512-byte packets.

2. PHASE 2: IEEE 802.11AX (WI-FI 6) OFDMA & MU-MIMO

2.1 Enhancements Over Baseline
In Phase 2, the identical physical topology from Phase 1 was maintained, but the underlying protocols were upgraded to the IEEE 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6) standard. The simulation leverages Multi-User Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MU-MIMO) and Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiple Access (OFDMA) to improve spectral efficiency and channel access. The physical layer was transitioned from YansWifiPhy to SpectrumWifiPhy to accurately model multi-bandwidth capabilities over a 40 MHz channel.

PHASE 3: UORA AND DENSE TOPOLOGIES

3. PHASE 3: UPLINK OFDMA RANDOM ACCESS (UORA)

3.1 Dense Network Topology
Phase 3 scales the network into a dense environment to evaluate advanced Wi-Fi 6 resource allocation. The network consists of 1 AP and 40 STAs. Nodes are placed using a random distribution model between 5 and 30 meters from the AP. A 3D spatial dimension is introduced, fixing the AP at a height of 3 meters and the STAs at 1 meter. The physical layer is upgraded to SpectrumWifiPhy operating on a 5GHz band with a 40MHz channel bandwidth.
